  Bahrain's PDPA Ministerial Order 43/2022 and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025 establish partially equivalent but materially different data breach notification frameworks. Both require notification to regulatory authorities within 72 hours and mandate detailed breach documentation. However, Bahrain provides three significant exemptions from Data Subject notification (encrypted data, disproportionate effort, and risk mitigation) that India's framework does not appear to recognize, making India's regime stricter for individual notification obligations. India imposes more granular content requirements for authority notifications, including mandatory identification of breach causation and responsible parties. Bahrain uniquely requires controllers to establish breach reporting channels for Data Subjects and grants the Authority discretionary override power on exemptions. Key compliance risks include: (1) India's apparent lack of encryption exemptions requiring notification even for encrypted breaches; (2) Bahrain's stricter 72-hour deadline with no discretionary extension; and (3) India's requirement to identify breach causation and responsible parties. Organizations operating in both jurisdictions should implement the stricter requirements as baseline compliance.
